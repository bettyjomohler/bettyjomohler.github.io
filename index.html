About

I am a scientist working at the intersection of extended reality (XR), human perception and machine learning. For more than two decades I have studied how people perceive space, motion and their own bodies in virtual and augmented reality, and how self-avatars change what we see and feel. I led an independent Max Planck research group in Tübingen, held a tenured W2 professorship at TU Darmstadt, and spent eight years as a Principal Applied Scientist at Amazon, building multimodal datasets, generative models, benchmarks and AR virtual try-on.

My work today focuses on bringing AI safely and measurably to people: in clinical assessment and therapy with partners at the University Hospital Tübingen, and in schools, where young people are the first generation to grow up learning with AI helpers. Every week I teach robotics and coding to children and teenagers in Tübingen.

Research

Medium

Extended reality & wearables

VR, AR, smart glasses and body-worn sensors as the way people meet AI, and as precise instruments for measuring perception, movement and behavior.

Method

Human-centered machine learning

Multimodal behavioral data and human-in-the-loop benchmarks that show whether generative and multimodal models actually work for people.

Impact

Clinics & classrooms

3D body and motion models for stroke and eating-disorder assessment, and research on how AI and wearables shape students’ learning, fitness and sense of self.
